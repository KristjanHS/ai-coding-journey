# 91 · the close

## What I was trying to do

Turn the journey into something teachable. The chapters are a record of one person's route; a course has
to be a route someone else can walk, which means it cannot be a tour of what I built. It has to be about
the decisions — what to govern, what to measure, when to stop.

The skeleton at [`/course/skeleton/`](/course/skeleton/) is the result: seven modules, one learning
outcome each, and for every module the chapters it draws its evidence from.

## What didn't work

The first framing was "how to code with AI", and it collapsed on contact with the audience. Roughly half
the intended learners have no software background, so a module that opens by editing a file has already
lost them, and the parts they would actually decide on — what a review system has to catch, what a
budget buys — never get reached.

Reframing it as governance and planning fixed the audience problem and exposed a content problem: three
of the seven modules had no artifact behind them, only opinion. Those are the modules the remaining
chapters exist to supply.

## What I learned

The binding constraint on a course like this is not the material, it is the evidence. Every module's
outcome had to be traceable to a chapter that shows something — a rule, a prompt, a defect a reviewer
caught, a measured number — because a claim with no artifact is exactly what this audience is already
drowning in.

That constraint runs backwards into the writing: the chapters are shaped by which modules need evidence,
not the other way round.

## Artifact

The rule the whole skeleton is built on, from its own opening:

```text
Every module draws its evidence from chapters of this journey,
so nothing is asserted that the repo cannot show.
```

The test of it is module 5, which asks the learner to distinguish a check that passes from a check that
*could have failed* — and to demand the demonstration. That is the one habit from this whole journey
that transfers to any job, with or without an agent.
